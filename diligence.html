Quantified investor diligence · August 2026

What has to be true for Enhaus to become a large, profitable company?

The thesis becomes investable when it can be expressed as measurable economics: enough addressable episodes, enough provider spread, low enough acquisition cost, repeatable network density and an insurance attachment that adds margin without forcing Enhaus to retain early underwriting risk.

Conclusion

Three revenue engines can share one infrastructure layer.

Care transactions: transparent episodes with contracted providers. Navigation/software: routing infrastructure for employers, providers and embedded partners. Carrier-backed protection: producer/MGA/admin economics on future accident risk. The moat is the shared intake + provider graph + routing + outcome dataset, not the insurance policy itself.

02 · Provider economics

There is visible room between retail price and payer reimbursement.

Enhaus only earns attractive care margins if it creates enough provider value to contract efficiently. The pitch is not “take less money”; it is “receive incremental, well-routed demand with lower acquisition/admin friction and greater payment certainty.”

03 · Insurance economics

Supplemental accident insurance can be profitable — but Enhaus should monetize distribution first.

Aflac's U.S. business provides a useful public benchmark: in 2025, benefits and claims were 47.3% of premiums, while insurance commissions were 8.2% of adjusted revenue and pretax adjusted margin was 20.6%. Accident represented 18.9% of U.S. new annualized premium sales. This does not predict Enhaus loss ratios, but it proves the category can support attractive economics at scale.

Why distribution economics are attractive

Insurance producers are generally compensated through a percentage of premium. Public carrier disclosures show commission ranges vary substantially by line; AIG's 2024 disclosures show many P&C lines in roughly the mid-teens to 20%+ range, while historical group-benefit brokerage disclosures show lower ranges.

Enhaus should negotiate its actual carrier economics rather than assume a generic MGA commission. Until then, model producer/MGA revenue as a sensitivity.

04 · Charlotte launch

A pilot should answer the questions that actually determine valuation.

Charlotte is large enough to generate meaningful volume and concentrated enough to build provider density. The objective is not “launch an insurance company.” It is to prove the care engine, then show that insurance and employer distribution improve lifetime economics.
