Investor diligence · quantified opportunity

From a compelling thesis to an underwritable business model.

This page pressure-tests the Enhaus opportunity with a bottom-up market model, provider reimbursement benchmarks, supplemental-insurance economics and a quantified Charlotte launch. Where hard public data exist, they are sourced. Where Enhaus has no operating history yet, assumptions are labeled explicitly and shown as sensitivities rather than facts.

Investment conclusion

The strongest version of Enhaus has three economic engines — and only one needs insurance.

Engine 1: care transactions. Aggregate consumers and capture economics between a transparent episode price and contracted provider cost. Engine 2: software/navigation. Sell access, routing and outcomes infrastructure to employers, providers and embedded partners. Engine 3: carrier-backed protection. Earn distribution/admin economics on future accident risk without putting Enhaus's early balance sheet behind claims. The strategic advantage is that all three engines share the same intake, provider graph, routing system and outcomes dataset.

Bottom-up obtainable-market framework

Annual addressable episodes = metro population × annual MSK help-seeking rate × Enhaus-addressable share Enhaus episode revenue = completed episodes × net revenue per episode Do not hard-code the help-seeking rate until pilot data exist.

Instead of claiming a fabricated SAM, management should report sensitivity. At 2.94M residents, every 1 percentage point of annual population penetration equals roughly 29,400 users. Every 0.1 percentage point equals ~2,940 users.

That makes the early goal measurable: prove conversion and contribution economics at fractions of one percent of a single metro before extrapolating nationally.
02 · Provider economics

The contracting wedge is visible in public benchmarks.

The provider side matters because Enhaus cannot create attractive episode economics merely by adding a consumer markup. It needs a network value proposition: predictable demand, faster payment, lower acquisition/admin burden, appropriate routing and potentially hybrid follow-up.

Luna publicly lists $135/visit self-pay in Charlotte. StrataPT reports aggregate Humana NC reimbursement benchmarks of $83.25 mean and $73.25 median. These are not apples-to-apples provider contracts, but they demonstrate meaningful price dispersion.

What Enhaus has to prove to providers

A provider may rationally accept a lower contracted rate if Enhaus delivers enough value elsewhere: incremental filled capacity, low/no marketing CAC, lower denial risk, faster payment, reduced scheduling friction and better-fit patients.

Provider value = contracted reimbursement + avoided CAC + avoided admin + capacity utilization + collection certainty

The negotiation should therefore focus on net provider economics, not simply “discount your rate.” The provider network becomes stronger if Enhaus can quantify the cost it removes from practices.
