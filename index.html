The investment thesis: Enhaus does not need to beat insurers at underwriting comprehensive health risk. It can own the high-frequency MSK front door, compress episode cost through routing and contracted care, monetize transactions and employer/provider access, and attach carrier-backed accident protection where risk is genuinely insurable.

Why this market is structurally attractive

Low back/neck pain alone represented $134.5B of U.S. spending in 2016; other MSK disorders represented another $129.8B. Private insurance funded the majority of both categories. Employer data continue to identify MSK as a major cost center.

For Enhaus, the opportunity is not merely the PT visit. It is the expensive path around it: PCP visits, delayed care, unnecessary downstream utilization, fragmented referrals, and opaque pricing.

Sources: JAMA health-spending analysis; UnitedHealthcare employer cost research; BEA/FRED.

Evidence for a cheaper front door

A 2021 U.S. systematic review found direct-access PT associated with lower PT costs, lower total health-care costs, fewer PT visits and greater functional improvement versus physician-first pathways. A 59,670-person claims study found PT-first care in unrestricted-access states associated with 13% lower costs at 30 days and 32% lower costs at 90 days versus PCP-first.

A 2026 systematic review covering 90,401 patients found direct access associated with 10–30% fewer GP consultations, generally neutral-to-lower episode costs, and no serious adverse events attributable to direct access in the studies reporting safety.

Sources: Hon et al. 2021; Garrity et al. 2020; Fischer et al. 2026.

Enhaus changes the value chain

Consumer describes problem→ AI structures intake→ deterministic safety screen→ right provider / modality→ contracted episode→ outcomes captured→ risk + routing data improve

The AI is not the medical product and should not diagnose. Its economic job is reducing search friction, collecting structured intent, identifying eligibility and matching a user to the lowest-cost clinically appropriate pathway.

Why not simply become an insurer?

Post-symptom insurance is structurally weak.

If a customer buys only after knowing they need $800 of PT, expected utilization is already visible. Sustainable premium converges toward expected claims + administration + margin. That is prepaid care, not attractive pooled risk.

Future accidental injury is different.

Enroll broad populations before injury and a carrier can pool uncertain risk. Enhaus can then monetize distribution while steering covered members into its efficient care network.

Proof that MSK can support venture-scale economics

Hinge Health reported $587.9M of 2025 revenue, up from $390.4M in 2024, with $468.2M gross profit and an 80% GAAP gross margin. Q4 2025 gross margin reached 84%. Hinge is not evidence that Enhaus's proposed insurance structure works; it is evidence that an MSK access/care platform sold at scale can produce substantial revenue and high gross margins.

Source: Hinge Health 2025 Form 10-K and FY2025 results.

What could make Enhaus defensible

1. Demand aggregation. Become the consumer entry point before provider selection.

2. Provider graph. Know specialty, price, insurance, geography, availability and outcomes.

3. Routing data. Learn which pathway produces the best outcome at the lowest episode cost.

4. Embedded distribution. Employers, sports, gyms and memberships lower CAC and create healthier insurance pools.

5. Contracting leverage. Volume enables episode pricing; episode pricing improves consumer certainty and margin visibility.

Investor risks that remain unresolved

Regulatory: state insurance, producer/MGA, TPA, corporate-practice, fee-splitting and provider-referral rules.

Clinical: triage must safely escalate red flags and avoid diagnostic claims.

Economics: provider rates, utilization, CAC, repeat use and claims incidence are not yet validated.

Marketplace: network density is required before matching creates consumer value.

Insurance: adverse selection can destroy the insured layer if enrollment occurs too near the loss event.
