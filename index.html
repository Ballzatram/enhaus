Core investment thesis

Enhaus does not need to beat insurers at underwriting. It needs to beat the healthcare system at routing.

The highest-leverage asset is the moment before provider selection. If Enhaus becomes the trusted entry point, it can influence care setting, provider, price, modality and escalation. Those choices determine episode economics. Insurance then becomes an attached product for future uncertainty rather than the mechanism Enhaus relies on to finance a known current condition.

01 · Why this market

Large spend is useful. Fragmentation is the opportunity.

MSK care combines five conditions that favor a new front door: high frequency, enormous spend, confusing consumer choice, meaningful price dispersion, and many cases that can begin conservatively without starting in a high-cost setting. Direct-access rules have also reduced the structural need for a physician gatekeeper, although state and payer restrictions still matter.

$134.5B

Low-back and neck pain alone

A major U.S. health-spending study estimated $134.5B in low-back/neck spending in 2016 and another $129.8B across other MSK disorders. Enhaus does not need to capture the clinical spend itself to build value; it needs to influence the path through it.

The TAM is not “PT visits.” It is the transaction and decision layer around MSK episodes.
$1,700+

Insured consumers can still shop like cash payers

The 2026 minimum deductible for an HSA-qualified self-only HDHP is $1,700. That means many consumers have insurance yet still care deeply about transparent pricing for a routine episode.

A simple price can be a product feature even when insurance exists.

The pieces exist. The combination is the whitespace.

04 · Insurance thesis

Insurance belongs after the care engine works.

Known pain is a poor risk pool: the customer already expects utilization. Future accidental injury is different. Enhaus can distribute carrier-backed accident coverage before injury, then route covered claims back into its care network when appropriate.

What the public market tells us

Aflac U.S. reported a 47.3% benefits-and-claims-to-premium ratio and 20.6% pretax adjusted margin in 2025 across its U.S. supplemental business. That does not predict Enhaus results, but it demonstrates that supplemental benefit pools can support profitable carrier economics.

Spot separately proves the embedded consumer pattern: activity-linked accident coverage can cover medical bills, including prescribed PT, while explicitly excluding general illness, pre-existing conditions and overuse.

05 · Regulatory architecture

Design the economics around the law, not after it.

North Carolina creates a workable path, but several lines matter: healthcare referral compensation, unauthorized clinical practice, insurance producer licensing, TPA administration and MGA thresholds. The most important early design decision is to keep Enhaus from accidentally becoming the regulated entity it does not yet need to be.

Green-lane launch architecture

Navigation softwareConsumer paymentProvider SaaSEmployer contractLicensed agencyCarrier risk

  • AI structures information but does not diagnose.
  • Licensed PTs own clinical evaluation and treatment decisions.
  • Enhaus can sell defined consumer services/episodes subject to healthcare-law structuring.
  • Insurance solicitation is handled through licensed producer/business-entity infrastructure.
  • Carrier retains underwriting risk initially.

Red-lane issues to avoid

  • Provider-paid referral fees: N.C. G.S. 90-401 prohibits health-care providers from financially compensating for recommending or securing their employment by a patient.
  • Practicing PT: the platform should not hold itself out as performing PT or medical diagnosis; licensed clinicians must own clinical services.
  • TPA creep: directly collecting premiums or adjusting/settling health-insurance claims can move Enhaus into TPA licensing territory.
  • MGA creep: underwriting substantial premium plus claims/reinsurance authority can trigger MGA regulation.
Practical implication: early provider monetization should emphasize SaaS, administrative services, contracted episode economics or advertising structures reviewed by counsel—not a simple “$X every time we refer a patient” model.
