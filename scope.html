Working scope · August 2026

Build the care engine first. Earn the right to add insurance.

This scope turns the Enhaus thesis into an executable program. The goal is not to launch every feature at once; it is to de-risk the few assumptions that determine whether Enhaus can become a large, profitable MSK transaction network.

North star

A trusted MSK front door that routes people to the right care, makes the economics understandable, and stays connected through the episode.

Every workstream should reinforce one of four assets: demand, provider density, transaction economics or outcome/routing data. Future accident protection is added only after Enhaus proves it can create value in the underlying care transaction.

01 · Workstreams

Eight tracks, one integrated product.

02 · Phase plan

Sequence the risk.

Each phase unlocks the next only when the underlying evidence is strong enough. This keeps Enhaus from spending capital on insurance or geographic scale before the care transaction works.

Phase 1 · Prove care engine

Charlotte MVP

  • 10–20 provider locations
  • working intake + safety routing
  • transparent cash episode
  • insurance-verification concept
  • 1,000–2,500 completed episodes
Gate: repeatable positive episode contribution + strong booking conversion
Phase 2 · Prove distribution

Employer + embedded

  • 2–5 employer/partner pilots
  • sports/gym/member channel
  • CAC by channel
  • provider density expansion
  • outcomes cohorts
Gate: B2B2C CAC and retention materially better than pure DTC
Phase 3 · Attach protection

Carrier-backed accident

  • licensed agency structure
  • carrier/administrator term sheet
  • future-injury enrollment
  • claims-routing integration
  • attachment + persistency data
Gate: insurance adds margin and retention without confusing the care proposition
Phase 4 · Expand network

Multi-market scale

  • second/third metro
  • standardized contracting playbook
  • enterprise integrations
  • provider scoring from outcomes
  • API distribution
Gate: economics replicate outside Charlotte

04 · Explicitly out of scope

What Enhaus should not build yet.

Early discipline is part of the strategy. These activities add capital, regulatory or clinical complexity before they create a defensible advantage.

No retained insurance risk at launch

A licensed carrier should hold policy reserves, underwriting and claim obligations until Enhaus has enough data and capital to justify considering otherwise.

No AI diagnosis

The model structures information and recommends a navigation pathway. Licensed clinicians diagnose and determine treatment within their scope.

No nationwide network before density

A shallow national directory is less valuable than a dense Charlotte network with real availability, economics and outcome data.

No simple provider referral bounty

North Carolina referral-compensation rules make a simplistic pay-per-patient provider model a poor starting architecture. Provider economics require healthcare-law counsel.

No internal claims administration by default

Use carrier/TPA infrastructure first. Only internalize regulated administration when the economics justify licensing and operational overhead.

No federal-program monetization assumptions

Commercial/self-pay should be the initial economic model until counsel addresses Medicare/Medicaid referral, billing and anti-kickback implications.
